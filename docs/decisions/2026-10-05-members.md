# Members: long-lived spawned agents

**Context.** Everything `tmux-spawn` starts is a sub agent, a temporary helper. In the agent chain, the main agent spawns the secondary and the worker with `tmux-spawn`, so they show up as sub agents. They're mixed into the `prefix + a` sub-agent list with temporary helpers and counted in the chip. `tmux-dismiss --done` and `^d` (clean done) can also close them, since they're `done` whenever they're idle. But they are long-lived chain agents, not helpers.

**Decision.** A second kind of spawned agent, a **member**. The name was chosen over "peer" or "sibling", because "peer" already means a connected agent in tmux-agents.

- **Spawning.** `tmux-spawn --member` works with `--split` and `--for`. A member is marked with the pane option `@member` and `member=1` in its session record.
- **Not a sub agent.**
  - Members don't appear in the sub-agent list; they show in the `^a` all-named-panes view.
  - The chip doesn't count them as sub agents.
  - `tmux-dismiss --done` and `^d` never close them.
- **Ownership is kept.** A member still has `@parent`, so `tmux-dismiss --from <main> <secondary>` closes the secondary, the worker and the worker's sub agents, as before.
- **Its own helpers stay sub agents.** Sub agents spawned by a member (e.g. the worker's helpers) are ordinary sub agents of that member.
- **Docs.** The agent-chain skill spawns the secondary and the worker with `--member`. The tmux-agents skill, the guide, both READMEs and the CHANGELOG describe members.

**Open questions (defaults in bold).**

1. Do members still get attention: needs-you and permission pinning in the chip, and in the list's "needs you" section? **Yes. Attention matters for long-lived agents too.**
2. How do members appear in the `^a` view? **As normal rows in their project group. PARENT shows their owner, prefixed with `member of`.**
3. Resume? **`tmux-spawn --resume` reopens a member as a member, from its record. Closed members are listed with closed sub agents in the closed section.**
4. Depth? **A member takes its owner's depth instead of adding a level, so the worker's helpers are depth 1 and can still start their own sub agents within the limit of 2.**
5. Turning a running agent into a member? **No conversion command. A chain that's already running keeps its sub agents until it's restarted.**

**Why.** Long-lived chain agents and short helpers need different handling: helpers are cleaned up in bulk, chain agents never are.

**Rejected.** Starting chain agents with `tmux-connect` as plain peers, which would lose ownership and the one-command `tmux-dismiss` of the whole chain. A separate members list, because the `^a` view already shows all named panes.
